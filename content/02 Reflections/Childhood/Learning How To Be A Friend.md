inviting a friend to come over.

cooking for them.

sharing my creations.

planning a date.

making a handmade gift.

these are things i learned i actually enjoy doing for people.

checking up on a friend when they’re going through something.

going to their birthday party.

meeting their friends.

trying to communicate clearly, even when it’s beyond my comfort zone.

these are efforts i learned are within my capacity to show my appreciation for people.

growing up destroyed a lot of the hope i had of building relationships. my experience of constantly moving houses and schools probably turned me into a bad friend. sadly, it took me many years as an adult to even understand that.

as an adolescent i was deeply hurt — that’s no news — but it upsets me that most people i have met will never know how profoundly i once cared.

unfortunately, leaving, ending chapters, closing off, shutting down, taking the french exit — or however you want to call it — was never difficult for me.

starting over is something i can’t imagine life without, but around the age of 12 or 13, when i was living in my sixth house and going to my seventh school, i decided to get back at the world (or so i believed) by not letting myself form any type of bond.

i won’t lie, it felt really good.

it felt empowering because the opposite of that — which was what i was constantly experiencing — was being torn into pieces whenever i had to leave without choosing to, knowing that the people i had left would never miss me in the same way i missed them.

they were still part of a reality that was already built for them, where other people existed, where everything was comfortable.

and what seemed to be reserved for me was the terror of introducing myself once more and having people decide whether they liked me or not all over again, putting in the effort to get to know them and, if those feelings were reciprocated, being vulnerable, repeatedly reminiscing about the past, only to end up in the same place where, eventually, i would leave and they would stay.

the last person i missed as a teenager was my best friend, jules, who left the school i was attending.

he actually did contradict a point i mentioned earlier in this text: that the person who stays does not miss the one who leaves in the same way.

i guess they don’t, but it can still be intense.

his absence left a huge mark on me, yet the sadness was shared, and maybe that is why he is the only friend i managed to keep in my life from that time.

one thing that always pissed me off was hearing, _“you’ll find other friends like him,”_ when he left.

i used to instantly think, _“no, you won’t.”_

you can never recreate a feeling between two people; it always feels a little different. a relationship between two beings who come together to create this whole new thing is irreplicable; it can never happen in the same way again.

and that’s how i saw and mourned the loss of my friend jules, or at least the version of him i used to have in my life, which i very much liked.

from that point on it’s been a lonelier ride.

jules is the last of the examples i could list from all the people who gained a place in my heart back then, who i tried to open up to, who i genuinely, childishly and enormously shared myself with.

i had people around me after that.

sure.

some of them liked me, others didn’t, but what i’m trying to say is that i rarely felt that deep connection, that ease that comes after you open up and realize you are accepted even with your worst flaws; the laughing until you feel your imaginary six-pack; the comfort of truly relying on someone in difficult times, or even trusting that i could be that person for someone else.

i really wasn’t in the position to be a good friend — or even a friend at all.

i had already poured everything i had, and i can tell you it wasn’t little, into all those other people i had met and left behind.

the ideal would have been for me to settle somewhere and be given the opportunity to learn how to nurture a friendship.

at least once would have been nice.

i was denied that, so i tried to deal with it, or at least protect myself from a complete breakdown.

i apologize for the passive aggression, but saying sorry for this does feel bittersweet to me.

the beauty of life is that two things can be true at once.

i understand people would eventually cut ties with me (to put it mildly) after i’d been around for a while, maybe gained a place in their lives, and then disappeared as if i had never known them at all.

sometimes i even blocked them, both online and offline, to avoid any emotional repercussions.

i understand how selfish that was.

as clear as all of this seems now, it wasn’t that clear back then, when i was too busy being a young girl trying to deal with what had been imposed on me.

i do feel like this choice — which i thought was so wise — backfired in multiple ways.

i feel sad for not having memories of _being a part of something_ for so many years, especially up to the age of 20.

i feel sad when i go back to my home country and have a ridiculously small number of friends to meet.

i feel sad that the last thought some people probably had of me wasn’t a good one.

i feel sad that, to this day, i still don’t really see another way of dealing with the constant moving i had to accept as a child.

i feel sad realizing this was the best i could do with the knowledge i had at the time, as therapists say.

today i am 29 years old.

understanding all of this and naming it — taking responsibility for it — does not make it disappear or make me a more social person.

how do i explain to people that i am only now learning how to be a friend?

how do i get over the fear of losing my individuality, which is one of the few positives that came out of all this?

how do i motivate myself to meet new people when, honestly, it feels like a drag until the second or third meeting?

can i stop making excuses for why i don’t like people anymore?

am i allowed to shape what i want these relationships to look like too?

or do we have to talk every week and i need to like all of your instagram posts?

to be honest — and to end this on a positive note — after years of questioning and digging, i have found something deep inside me that may help.

apart from a few strategies (which i listed at the beginning of this text) that make the possibility of being a friend feel real, remembering the innocent, sincere memories of childhood friends from before jules also comforts me.

i intend to keep those memories locked away safely inside me, even if i am never missed with the same intensity, even if i never have moments like those again, because i can create something new that is also beautiful.

i was part of those old relationships, and part of what made them possible still lives in me.

i am humbled by the possibility of living a life that extends beyond what i think i am, and open to growing through the experiences others share with me.
